🚇 Getting Around Sofia

  • Sofia city centre is easy to explore on foot.
  • Public transport is affordable and easy to use.
    - Single ticket: €0.80 (allows transfers within first 30 minutes)
    - 24-hour pass: €3.00
    - 72-hour pass: €7.60
    *You can pay directly with a contactless bank card or phone on many validators.
  • The metro is the fastest way to move around the city.
  • Most restaurants and shops accept cards.
  • Keep some cash for small shops and markets.
  • Taxis are available through local apps.
    - Yellow Taxi - reliable and widely used for everyday trips
    - TaxiMe - convenient app with easy booking and ride tracking
    - Volt - premium taxi service with more comfortable cars (higher price)
    *Always use official taxi services and avoid unmarked cars.

💡 Useful Local Tips

  • The currency is Euro (EUR).
  • Tap water is safe to drink.
  • Bulgarians traditionally shake their head for "yes" and nod for "no".
  • Public toilets may require a small fee in some places, so carrying a few coins can be useful.
  • Sofia is generally safe, but as in any capital city, keep an eye on your belongings in crowded areas and on public transport.

🍽️ Traditional Bulgarian food

  • Shopska salad — a traditional Bulgarian salad with tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers, onions and Bulgarian white cheese (sirene).

  • Bulgarian yogurt — famous for its thick texture and unique taste, made using the traditional yogurt cultures. Locals enjoy it with breakfast, honey, fruit, or alongside traditional meals.

  • Banitsa — a popular pastry made with filo dough, eggs and cheese. Perfect for breakfast with Bulgarian yogurt.

  • Kebapche — grilled minced meat rolls, usually served with fries, salad or lyutenitsa (a pepper and tomato spread).

  • Kavarma — a slow-cooked dish with meat, vegetables and spices, traditionally served warm in a clay pot.

  • Tarator — a cold yogurt, cucumber, garlic and dill soup, especially refreshing in summer.

  • Rakia — a traditional Bulgarian fruit spirit, usually enjoyed as an aperitif with a salad.

  • Bulgarian wine — try local varieties such as Mavrud, Melnik or Rubin.
